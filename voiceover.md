# Voiceover: Founder Tip Tuesday, week of October 20, 2026

1. It's Founder Tip Tuesday. The median Series A this year sold eighteen percent of the company, the lowest share in the last few years, according to Peter Walker's benchmarks at Carta. And the question he asked about those numbers is the one to bring into your next raise: how much cash do you really need?

2. Walker's sample was more than a thousand rounds raised in the six months before this July, all software companies on Carta cap tables, with bridges and extensions left out. So hardware founders should read his benchmarks as software numbers. Carta said its deep tech data was coming soon.

3. At seed, the median round raised four point one million dollars and sold eighteen percent of the company, a share Walker says has been ticking down for months.

4. At Series A, the median round raised fourteen point four million dollars, at a median valuation of eighty million.

5. Walker writes that valuation is often just the output of the other two numbers in a round: the cash raised, and the share of the company sold. You can check it on the Series A medians. Fourteen point four million is eighteen percent of eighty million.

6. That gives a founder two places to start. You can start from a valuation and raise whatever that price will carry. Or you can start from what the next milestone costs, and let the stake and the price follow from the cash.

7. The seed headlines pull founders toward a valuation. Carta's ninety-fifth percentile seed valuation reached two hundred point four million dollars in the second quarter, up from seventy-two point two million a year earlier.

8. But those prices sit on fewer deals. In past booms, the number of rounds rose along with the valuations. This time fewer rounds are being completed, with more money going into fewer companies. With fewer next rounds to go around, the cash you raise now has to carry you to something the next investor will fund.

9. So start on paper, with the next proof point you'll show an investor: a working unit in a customer's hands, a pilot that renews, or a first production run. Put a price on it in months and dollars. Work out the share of the company you'd sell to fund it last.

10. What will my next proof point cost, in months and in dollars?
